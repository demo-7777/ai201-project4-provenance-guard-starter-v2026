1. Writer sends JSON {text, creator_id} to POST /submit.
   The route rejects empty text, missing text, or a work 
   beyond the size limit, then it creates a content_id.


2. That text is passed to signal one where the data is
   read and given a score from 0-1 evaluating how predictable 
   the text is to a language model (gpt2). A score of 1 means has low
   perplexity.

3. That text is then passed to signal two where the data is
   read and given a score from 0-1 evalauting  the text's shape: 
   sentence length spread, word variety, punctuation. A score of 1 
   means the shape of the text is consistent with AI patterns.

4. The scores from each signal are passed to scoring.py and used 
   by combine_signals to generate a score that is a combination of 
   the two from 0-1. For now, the combining rule would be
   (score1+score2)/2 (until there is an observed valid reason to justify 
   one score having more weight than the other).

5. Given the combined score, that score would be read in scoring.py 
   by score_to_label to decide the guess and label to assign to the writer's 
   work. The guess is determined based on the thresholds set in config.py

6. The label and the work it corresponds to is recorded 
   by log_decision into logs/audit.jsonl.
   Other additional elements recorded include timestamp, content_id, 
   creator_id, guess, both signal scores, the combined score, the label, 
   and status decided.

7. The route sends back JSON with content_id, guess, confidence, label, 
   model_score and style_score. The writer sees the label, and the content_id
   is what lets them appeal.


False-positive trace
--------------------
Who: a writer whose first language isn't English submits a short personal
essay they wrote themselves. They stick to words they're sure of and keep
sentences short and similar in length.

1. POST /submit accepts the text (not empty, under the size limit) and
   creates a content_id.

2. Signal one: common words in short, ordinary sentences are easy for gpt2
   to predict -> low perplexity -> model_score around 0.80.

3. Signal two: sentence lengths barely vary and the vocabulary repeats ->
   style_score around 0.70.

4. combine_signals: (0.80 + 0.70) / 2 = 0.75.

5. score_to_label: 0.75 is above the AI threshold (0.65 for now) ->
   guess "ai", and the writer sees the high-confidence AI label on work
   they wrote themselves.

6. log_decision records the decision with status "decided".

7. The writer receives the label and their content_id.

8. The writer sends POST /appeal with their content_id and their reasoning
   (e.g. "I wrote this myself; I write simply because English is my second
   language").

9. The route checks the content_id exists, log_appeal records the appeal
   next to the original decision, and the status becomes "under_review".
   The writer gets a confirmation.

10. A person reviews the appeal and decides whether the label stays or
    changes. The system does not re-check automatically.

What this tells me: both signals fail the same way on this writer, so
averaging doesn't protect them. The threshold and the label wording are
what decide how bad this is for them, which makes the AI threshold the
number to set carefully.


Endpoint fields
---------------
POST /submit
  in:
    text        string, required. Non-empty, at most 10,000 characters
                (limit is a placeholder - decide before building).
    creator_id  string, required. Non-empty. Who wrote it.
  out (200):
    content_id   string. Unique id for this submission; needed to appeal.
    guess        "ai" | "human" | "unsure"
    confidence   number 0-1. Combined score; higher = more likely AI.
    label        string. The full sentence the writer/reader sees.
    model_score  number 0-1. Signal one's score.
    style_score  number 0-1. Signal two's score.
    status       "decided"
  error (400):
    error        "invalid_input"
    message      string. Which field is wrong and why
                 (missing, empty, too long, not a string).

POST /appeal
  in:
    content_id  string, required. Must match an earlier submission.
    reasoning   string, required. Non-empty. The writer's explanation.
  out (200):
    content_id  string. Same id that was appealed.
    status      "under_review"
    message     string. Confirmation that the appeal was recorded and a
                person will review it.
  error (400):
    error       "invalid_input"
    message     string. content_id or reasoning missing/empty.
  error (404):
    error       "not_found"
    message     string. No submission with that content_id.
